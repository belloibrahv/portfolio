Before I wrote software for a living, I knew the market. In a Lagos market, nobody reads your terms and conditions. They look at your face, then at the goods, and then they decide.

It took me years to see how much of that carried over.

## Say the true price

A customer can forgive a high price. They won't forgive a surprise. It's the same with a software estimate. "Seven weeks" that's honest is worth more than "three weeks" that everyone knows is a hope.

> People will pay for the truth. They just won't pay for it twice.

## The customer comes back

A trader thinks about next month, because the same person will walk past the same stall. Software is no different. Whoever uses the thing you shipped today will call you when it breaks. So you build it as if you'll have to look them in the eye.

## Know your stock

A good trader can tell you what's on the shelf without checking. An engineer should be able to do the same with their system: what's running, what's failing, and what happens when the network drops.

---

None of this is clever. It's just what happens when you've had to stand behind what you sold.
